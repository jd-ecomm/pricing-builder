About this report

This report brings together three Google Merchant Centre exports into a single view of JD Sports Canada's competitive pricing position. Every SKU is compared to its Market Price (the click-weighted median competitor price on Google Shopping) to identify where we're over- or under-priced relative to the market. For ~41% of SKUs Google has also run an AI-powered Discount Price Simulation, modelling how clicks and conversions would grow if we moved to a specific discounted price. Designed for weekly use — filter, search, and click any row for a visual price comparison. Ecomm Price is sourced directly from JD's Google product feed (effective price — sale if active, else regular) for live accuracy.

Dynamic SKU Pricing
83.6% of titles have multiple Market Prices across variants. Always work at Product ID level, not Title level.
A single style — e.g. Jordan 1 Mid SE — may carry 13 Product IDs (different sizes and colourways), each with a distinct competitive market price ranging from $138 to $368. This is how the resale and sneaker market works: size and colourway scarcity drives wildly different demand, and Google's benchmark reflects that. A "Nike Air Force 1 '07" sits at one market price for the common colourway and a very different one for a limited drop — treating them as one product would produce misleading pricing decisions.
Ecomm Price
Current effective price on JD Sports Canada for this Product ID — sale_price if set, else regular price. Sourced directly from the JD product feed.
Market Price
The typical competitor price on Google Shopping — a click-weighted median of what customers actually pay at competing retailers. The hero price for decision-making. Unique per Product ID.
Price Gap %
(Ecomm Price − Market Price) ÷ Market Price. Positive = priced above market (red). Negative = priced below market (green). At market = within ±2%.
Discount Price Simulation
Google's AI simulation of performance at a discounted price — NOT a recommendation. A thought exercise showing predicted click / conversion growth if we ran that discount.
Simulation Confidence
Google's confidence in its own simulation: High, Medium, Low. Based on data volume and signal strength for that product.
Click / Conv. Uplift %
Predicted percentage growth (not multiplier) in clicks and conversions at the simulated discount price. e.g. +198% means clicks nearly triple.

Data notes. Ecomm Price is sourced from JD's Google product feed (TSV export), using the effective price (sale_price when set, else regular price). SKUs marked with a red "S" tag have an active sale_price in the feed. The Price Changes tab tracks effective price movements between feed snapshots. Market Price = Google click-weighted median competitor price, unique per Product ID to reflect size and colourway scarcity. Simulation coverage is approximately 41% of the catalogue; only SKUs with a simulation show uplift columns. Uplift values are percentage growth (Google's native format), not multipliers — e.g. +198% means clicks nearly triple. "Your price" sourced from the Price Benchmarks export (canonical); the Sale Price Suggestions export showed 1,249 price discrepancies, reconciled to the Benchmarks value. 1,249 Google sale-price suggestions reference Product IDs that are not present in the current Price Benchmarks export and are excluded from this view — likely delisted SKUs or variants that fell out of Google's benchmark tracking this window. Single-product elasticity — Google's simulations do not model cannibalisation between our own SKUs, so sum-of-simulations should be treated as a ceiling. Last-click Google Shopping only — Meta and Klaviyo contribution to the same purchases is not visible here.
